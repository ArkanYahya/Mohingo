# UX Principles

## Voice & tone

Warm, encouraging, celebratory — never scolding. A learner getting an answer wrong is treated as a normal step, not a failure ("Not quite — here's why" rather than "Wrong"). All user-facing copy is bilingual (Arabic/English) and each language version is written natively for that language's tone, not machine-translated from the other.

## Error messages

- Format/pattern: what happened, in plain language, plus what to do next — no raw exception text or error codes shown to the learner.
- Exercise-level "errors" (wrong answers) are not treated as app errors — they use the correct/incorrect exercise feedback pattern (see [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md)), not an error message.
- True app errors (e.g. sync failure, offline when online was required) explain the cause simply ("Couldn't sync your progress — we'll try again when you're back online") and never block the learner from continuing offline work.

## Accounts and sync

- **Sign-in is an offer, never a gate.** Nothing asks for an account on launch or before a lesson; Settings → Account & sync says what an account is for ("keep your progress safe and carry on from another phone") and offers Sign in. The sign-in screen says the learner can keep learning without one.
- **Sign-in errors** follow the error rule above — one plain sentence per cause (`AuthFailure` → `authError*` strings), never a Firebase code. A wrong email and a wrong password read the same ("The email or password is incorrect."), and the password-reset confirmation reads the same whether or not an account exists — neither reveals who has an account. Closing the Google sheet is a choice, not an error: nothing is shown.
- **Sync is a status, not an alert.** The account row's subtitle says Syncing…, Last synced (date and time), or — never as a dialog or banner — that the phone is offline and the progress is saved here, that it'll try again, or that the sign-in expired; plus how many changes are waiting. A learner mid-lesson never sees any of it.
- **The guest-progress question** is asked once, right after a first sign-in on a phone that has progress: "Add this phone's progress to your account?" — Add to my account / Keep it on this phone. Both answers keep everything; closing the dialog leaves the question open on the account row, and nothing syncs until it is answered.
- **Signing out** confirms first and says what happens: the phone shows guest progress until the account signs in again, and — only if some progress hasn't uploaded yet — that it stays on this phone and syncs next time.
- **Reset while signed in** names every device in both the row ("on all your devices") and the confirmation ("on this phone and on every other phone signed in to your account"), per the destructive-action rule.

## Explanations and grading signals

- **A grade is never explained by a number.** The meaning signal and the rest of the grading signals ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-on-device-ai-for-free-answers.md)) change a score within a bound; the learner sees the same Correct / Not quite / Answer saved panel as before. Nothing on screen says "meaning 0.8".
- **A generated explanation is labelled.** When the AI pack has written one, the panel adds "Explanation" / "الشرح", the English, the Arabic when there is one, and always "Generated on your phone" / "مُولَّد على هاتفك" under it. It sits *below* the verdict and never contradicts it: the verdict is the grade, the explanation is help. Each language is the model's own writing, never a translation of the other, and if the model produced only English the Arabic line is left out rather than filled in.
- **Waiting is named for where it happens.** An answer queued for the online grammar check says "we'll finish checking this once you're online" (ADR 0005); one queued only for the installed pack says "checking this on your phone" (`activityFeedbackPendingLocalJudgement`), because telling a learner to go online for something that needs no network would be wrong twice.
- **The Settings row says what it does, not what it is.** "Smart feedback" / "ملاحظات ذكية": a one-time download that explains your answers, on your phone, and that nothing leaves it. Never "AI", never a model's name — the same rule as the sync row, which never says Firestore.

## Empty states

Placement test not yet taken / no progress yet: framed as an invitation to start, not a blank/broken screen ("Let's find your starting point" rather than "No data").

Lesson answer review, per-activity: a finished lesson's read-only review screen (Learn tab only) can encounter an activity with no recorded attempt at all — content changed after the lesson was completed, for instance. This renders inside that activity's own chrome (never a generic list item or a raw gap) as a neutral "Not answered" placeholder — no icon or color implying an error, since nothing went wrong; the learner simply never answered it.

## Loading states

Content downloads (new/updated lesson content) show progress, since they may take time on a slow connection; local/cached content renders instantly with no loading state since it's already on-device (this is the offline-first point).

## Confirmations / destructive actions

Confirmation required only for actions that lose progress or data (e.g. resetting progress in Settings, which erases all local mastery evidence and attempt history — see [DATA_MODEL.md](DATA_MODEL.md)). Confirmation copy states what will be lost, plainly, before the action is taken. Retaking the placement test is **not** destructive under the free-access model (see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) flow 5) — it's advisory only and never erases existing unit progress, so it needs no confirmation.

## Tests

The placement test and final assessment never tell the learner whether an answer was right while the test is running — no colours, sounds or correct answers, and no listening transcripts — because both are there to measure, and the assessment's items must stay unseen for a retake. Say so before the test starts (the intro's rules note) and after each answer ("Answer saved · You'll see your results at the end"). The final mission is a scenario the learner works through, so it keeps normal right/wrong feedback.

## Levels

A level that isn't open right now always says why, in the same bottom sheet, and always offers a way forward — the app never presents a level the learner can't act on at all without explaining itself. A level the learner hasn't reached yet names what it builds on and offers two ways forward: take the placement test, or open it anyway, since progression is a recommendation, never a wall (see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) flow 5). A level the app hasn't released yet says so plainly and offers only to look at what it covers, since there's genuinely nothing to open. See [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md)'s Level lock sheet.

Browsing an unreleased level never implies the learner has moved into it or is enrolled in it: the "your course" overline and the current-unit emphasis are both left out while browsing, and its units render read-only, with a clear way back to the level the learner is actually in.

**Looking at another level is a view, never a move.** A screen that follows the learner's level opens on it, and where that screen is for looking things up rather than working — Words & phrases, Grammar — it offers the other levels as a plain filter. Picking one shows that level's items and nothing else changes: the learner is still in their own level, and nothing is announced as if they had switched. This is what stops "follow the current level" becoming a loss — an A2 learner still needs A1's words and forms, which are still in spaced review and still turn up in A2's texts, and taking them away would be worse than the bug of never showing A2's. Level codes on such a control stand alone and so may be bare, but the moment a code is spoken or written into a sentence — a screen-reader label included — an Arabic noun precedes it (`المستوى A2`).

Finishing a level is never a dead end, and moving on is never automatic. Once every unit of the learner's level is mastered and the level that builds on it is open, Home offers it — "Start A2" / "ابدأ المستوى A2", with why it's being offered and the plain statement that the moment is theirs to choose ("move when you're ready"). The app does not switch them across: the lock design only ever recommends an order, and silently relabelling someone's course would be the app deciding for them. Before that, no offer appears at all — a learner mid-level is not nagged about the next one.

**Some things belong to the learner, not to a level.** Following the current level is the rule for everything that shows a *course* — Home, Learn, Tests, the capstones — but the Review tab deliberately shows every level the learner has reached at once, and must keep doing so. Spaced review exists precisely so an A2 learner keeps meeting A1's words while A2 leans on them; narrowing the queue to the current level would quietly stop reviewing them at the moment they matter most. The consequence for copy: anything Review names by unit says which level's unit it means — "Level A2 · Unit 1" / `المستوى A2 · الوحدة 1` — since the learner is looking at two levels' unit numbers in one list. And the count on a card is always the count of what tapping it actually opens: promising "3 words due" and then showing 2 is the app losing track of the learner, the same failure as telling them they are studying A1 when they are studying A2.

**When the course changes under a learner, say so once and change nothing else.** Content improves after people have studied it, and sometimes an activity they answered is no longer in the unit, or the unit now asks for different Can-Do objectives. The unit says "Updated since you last studied it" / "تغيّر محتوى هذه الوحدة منذ آخر مرة" — added *below* whatever the row already said, never instead of it, because the learner still mastered that unit and is still owed that. It is not an error, not their fault and not a warning: their progress is kept, the unit stays as open as any other, and nothing asks them to redo anything. A unit whose content grew — a new activity, a new objective — says nothing at all; only a unit that lost something the learner had actually worked on does, so the message stays rare enough to mean something. The Arabic is written as Arabic and carries no second-person verb, which would otherwise force a gender the app does not know.

**Copy never hard-codes a level name.** A string that names the level the learner is in takes it as a parameter, in both languages — the alternative is telling someone studying A2 that they are studying A1, which reads as the app having lost track of them. In Arabic an Arabic noun always precedes the Latin code — `المستوى {level}`, never a bare `{level}` at a sentence boundary. All twelve strings that broke this rule are fixed. Seven went first: `learnFinishOverline` ("Finish A2" / "ختام المستوى A2"), `testsHubSubtitle`, `testsFinishOverline`, `testsMissionBody`, `finalAssessmentIntroBody`, `assessmentResultPassedTitle` and `homeAllUnitsMastered` ("You've mastered every A2 unit so far!" / "أتقنت جميع وحدات المستوى A2 حتى الآن!") all take the level as a parameter now, so Home, the Learn tab, the Tests tab and both capstones name the level the learner is actually in. The same sweep took the descriptor beside the level code on Home's header off A1: `homeLevelBadge` now takes one (`cefrDescriptorLabel`, `lib/core/l10n/`), because "A2 · Beginner" undoes what finishing A1 meant — A1 is "Beginner"/"مبتدئ", A2 is "Elementary"/"أساسي", and a level with no descriptor yet shows its bare code rather than borrowing another level's. The four placement ones went the same way — `placementIntroTitle`, `placementIntroBody`, `placementResultMasteredTitle` and `testsPlacementResultMastered` (that last one renders on the Tests tab, in the placement row) — along with every placement string that names a unit, since the test now spans two levels and "Unit 5" alone no longer says which. A placement result reads "A2 Unit 5 is where to begin" / "الوحدة 5 من المستوى A2 هي نقطة البداية", and the level a result names is always one the app has actually released: it can say a level looks mastered, never that the learner is past the last level that exists. The twelfth and last, `settingsProfileMeta` (Settings' profile card), followed in `fc8fe56`, so the rule is now kept everywhere: **no string in `app_en.arb`/`app_ar.arb` names a level literally.** Treat any level name you see in either file as a bug to fix, not a pattern to copy.

## Accessibility baseline

- Standard: WCAG 2.1 AA
- Non-negotiables:
  - All interactive elements screen-reader labeled in both Arabic and English (VoiceOver/TalkBack)
  - Color contrast ratios met in both light and dark themes (see [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) tokens)
  - Correct/incorrect exercise feedback is never conveyed by color alone — always paired with an icon (check/X) and text
  - Full RTL mirroring for Arabic — layout, icons with directional meaning (e.g. back arrows), and gestures all flip correctly. Curriculum text is the exception that proves it: an English line always reads from the left edge and an Arabic line from the right, whichever interface language is on (see [DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](DESIGN_SYSTEM.md), Direction) — learners on the Arabic interface read an English title flush right as a mistake, not as mirroring
