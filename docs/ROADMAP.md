# Roadmap

## Vision

A self-directed adult Arabic-speaking learner can go from zero English (A1) to full proficiency (C2), through the same structured, level-by-level, four-skill progression established for the MVP — offline-first, bilingual, on one app.

## Content phases (A1 → C2)

Guided-learning-hour figures below are cumulative, standard CEFR/Cambridge estimates (from zero) — a guideline for scoping each phase's content volume, not a commitment to exact numbers.

| Phase | CEFR level(s) | Cumulative guided hours (reference) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVP | A1 | ~70–100 | Feature-complete, Phase 10 (QA/performance/store-readiness/docs) in progress — see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) and [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) |
| Phase 2 | A2 | ~160–200 | **Done** — all six steps of the level-design spec have landed (app foundations, current level + level lock, import, pilot unit, four authoring batches, release, and step 2b's six parts ending with the placement test). Every tab now shows the learner the level they are in, Review spans all of them, and placement covers both. What A2 carries into B1, rather than owes: its Arabic was read and confirmed by the project's Arabic-speaking owner on 2026-09-21 (a human read is a release step at every level, since no gate can judge whether a line reads naturally), and passing a Final Assessment still doesn't mark a level complete — see [ADR 0006](adr/0006-multiple-levels.md) and the [A2 level-design spec](superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-a2-level-design.md) |
| Phase 3 | B1 | ~350–400 | Not started |
| Phase 4 | B2 | ~500–600 | Not started |
| Phase 5 | C1 | ~700–800 | Not started |
| Phase 6 | C2 | ~1,000–1,200 | Not started |

Each content phase repeats the same shape as A1: break the level into thematic blocks derived from CEFR "can-do" statements for that level, author blocks/units/exercises across all 4 skills through the AI-generate-then-review pipeline, and ship the result as a new bundled asset (`assets/content/en/A2/...`, etc.) the same way A1 ships — no Firestore, no network sync, per the local-only v1 decision (see [ADR 0004](adr/0004-local-only-v1-no-firebase-backend.md)); the app-owned level catalogue (`kLevelCatalog` in `lib/content/catalog/level_catalog.dart`) is what makes adding a level purely additive there. But "just more content" understated what A2 actually needed first: a level building on an earlier one (so its lessons can reference A1 grammar/words/phrases), per-item level lookup (a screen finds an item's level from its id instead of assuming A1), and rendering for content fields the app had parsed but never shown — real app mechanics, landed as the A2 spec's step 1 ("app foundations") on `feature/a2-foundations`, followed by step 2a ("current level, level lock") on `feature/a2-current-level` — a learner-facing current level, a soft lock, and a browsable-but-closed preview, all for the Learn tab only.

A2's *content* phase understated its own work too. What the curriculum package produced fit A1's data contract but was not a course: one-line conversations, no present perfect anywhere in the level CEFR is named for, 80 listening activities against 232 reading, and 120 productive tasks whose `evaluation` was a retired rubric-name list the parser drops to `null` — so no speaking, writing or mediation answer could ever be graded. Steps 3-6 (`feature/a2-pilot-u1`) rewrote it unit by unit in the shipped asset, added the gates that keep it rewritten (then `test/content/a2_*`; since 2026-09-22 one suite in `test/content/levels/` over every level, which A1 was brought up to the same day), and flipped the catalogue to `available`. Budget a comparable authoring effort for B1, not a copy-and-level-up: what the generator produces is a starting point, not a shippable level.

What release did **not** cover, and what Phase 2 therefore still owes: the rest of step 2b, the rest of the multiple-levels UI. Home, the Tests tab and both capstones now follow the current level (`currentCourseProvider`), so A2's Final Mission and Final Assessment — in the bundle and gated by `test/content/levels/level_capstones_test.dart` — are reachable, an A2 learner opens the app to A2's continue card and A2's journey, and the seven level-naming strings those screens render take the level as a parameter. Home also closes the dead end the level lock created: a learner who has mastered every unit of their level is offered the next one, as a card they tap rather than a switch the app makes. Words & phrases and Grammar follow it too, with the A1/A2 filter the design called for, so an A2 learner reaches the 462 words and phrases A2 teaches and its 74 grammar forms without losing sight of A1's. The Review tab followed too — but *not* onto the current level: by design it spans every level the learner has reached at once, since an A2 learner's A1 words are exactly the ones spaced review exists for. Its due count and its review session are now one list, so the tab can no longer count an A2 word it then can't show, and its weak spots come from every level with a unit in progress, each labelled with its level. The placement test was the last of it, and the one screen that belongs to no level: it now passes over every released level in blocks of three or four units, one question per unit, and only opens up a block it fails — three questions per unit, stopping at the first that fails. A complete beginner answers 7 questions instead of 39; a learner who knows both levels, 25. Its result can start someone in A2 (making that level current and unlocking it), and "the top released level looks mastered" is the most it will ever claim. Results stored before the change — `a1Mastered` and friends — still read in history. `settingsProfileMeta` — the twelfth and last string that said "A1" whatever level the learner was in — took the level as a parameter the same day (`fc8fe56`), so no ARB string hard-codes a level name now. Phase 2's last open item closed on 2026-09-21, when the project's Arabic-speaking owner read units 3-12's Arabic and confirmed it. See [ADR 0006](adr/0006-multiple-levels.md) for what's decided and landed, and the [A2 level-design spec](superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-a2-level-design.md) for the step-2b design. B1+ inherit the mechanism ADR 0006 establishes — each just declares the level it builds on — so later phases shouldn't need to repeat this app-mechanics step, only the content-authoring shape described above.

## Non-content roadmap (deferred from MVP)

These were explicitly scoped out of the MVP (see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md)) and are revisited here as the product matures, roughly in this order:

1. ~~**Cloud-based AI grading for speaking/writing**~~ — **replaced by on-device grading, 2026-10-22** ([ADR 0013](adr/0013-on-device-ai-for-free-answers.md)). The owner's rule is no paid AI API, ever, so the cloud path was never going to be taken; instead a free answer is graded by its rubric plus a bounded meaning signal from a bundled embedding model (built: every keyword-graded mastery speak/write task now has a model answer to compare with), and an optional downloadable AI pack adds judgement and explanations (item 12).
2. **CI/CD pipeline** — **built 2026-10-23**, as far as a build goes: GitHub Actions runs the checks on every push and produces an installable Android APK and an unsigned iOS build (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`, [TESTING.md](TESTING.md) "CI"). Still owed: release signing (a real Android keystore held as a repository secret, an Apple developer account for iOS) and store upload.
3. **Monetization** — no plan yet; revisit once there's a working, validated product across more than one level.
4. **Social/community features** — streaks-with-friends, leaderboards, etc. — only after the core solo learning experience is proven.
5. **Teacher/classroom tools** — a different user (teacher) and data model (groups/assignments); treat as a distinct sub-project with its own brainstorming pass, not a bolt-on.
6. **Additional native languages beyond Arabic** — would require generalizing the currently-fixed `nativeLanguage`/`targetLanguage` fields (already schema-flexible, see [DATA_MODEL.md](DATA_MODEL.md)) into a real localization system, plus re-authoring contrastive explanations per language pair.
7. **Analytics and crash reporting** — no telemetry SDK ships in v1, to keep the app free of any required network call or third-party data collection while it's fully local. Revisit once there's a concrete question telemetry would answer.
8. **Account-based sync — built 2026-09-23; local backup export/import still deferred.** Optional Firebase accounts copy a signed-in learner's progress between devices ([ADR 0010](adr/0010-optional-accounts-and-progress-sync.md)). A guest still has no way to move progress except by signing in; a file export/import stays deferred.
9. **On-demand audio packs** — every level's speech clips ship inside the app today (~50 MB for A1 and A2, [ADR 0008](adr/0008-bundled-pre-generated-speech-audio.md)). When the total would cross Google Play's and Apple's 200 MB mobile-data thresholds — around the fourth level at the current 60 MB budget — move later levels' clips to Play Asset Delivery / Background Assets, behind the same `AudioClipIndex`. A content follow-up sits next to this: A2's two-person listening transcripts carry no speaker labels, so they play in one voice; marking turns (`Name: …`) in the bundle lets the tool render them per speaker with no app change.
10. **What accounts still owe the stores.** (a) **In-app account deletion** — built ([ADR 0011](adr/0011-first-run-agreement-app-guide-and-account-deletion.md)); the web page the Play listing links to is live at `https://mohingo.web.app/delete-account` (built by `tool/legal/`, deployed with `firebase deploy --only hosting`). (b) **iOS**: **Sign in with Apple** (App Store guideline 4.8) is built, with Apple token revocation on account deletion ([ADR 0012](adr/0012-ios-release-sign-in-with-apple.md)); the iOS app is registered in Firebase with its Google URL scheme, and an unsigned build compiles with Xcode 26.6 (2026-09-24); still to do on the owner's accounts: the Apple provider and its key ([FIREBASE_SETUP.md](FIREBASE_SETUP.md), iOS), Apple Developer membership, and a run on an iPhone. Later: Sign in with Apple on Android (Apple's web flow), so an Apple account can follow the learner to an Android phone. (c) Nice-to-have: live updates while two devices are open at once (today a device picks up the other's changes on launch, on returning to the foreground, every 10 minutes, or on Sync now).

11. **iPad layout.** 1.0.0 ships iPhone only (`TARGETED_DEVICE_FAMILY = 1`): on an iPad it runs in a phone-sized window. Supporting iPad means checking every screen at iPad sizes and in split view, and adding 13-inch iPad screenshots to the listing ([STORE_LISTING.md](STORE_LISTING.md)).
12. **Phase B as decided by [ADR 0014](adr/0014-read-free-answers-by-parts-with-nli.md) (2026-10-24).** The language-model pack of ADR 0013 was tried on a phone (`tool/ai_pack_spike/results/`: Gemma 3 1B, LFM2.5 1.2B, Qwen2.5 1.5B — none read a learner's answer reliably or found a grammar mistake, at 6–18 s and 1.2–2.2 GB) and not chosen. Built instead, the same day: every `keywordChecklist` group carries a *meaning* (a statement the bundled MobileBERT-MNLI model reads the answer against, 28 ms and 25 MB on the spike, and the part's bilingual name), the meaning signal is the share of groups met by words or by meaning, and the feedback names the parts an answer left out, templated from the content in both languages. **Owed:** (a) the model files themselves in `assets/models/nli/` — Hugging Face is unreachable from the development environment, so the owner runs `tool/meaning/fetch_nli_model.sh` and commits; until then the app grades by the similarity fallback exactly as before; (b) ~~A2's group meanings~~ done the same day: A2's 473 mastery-task groups are authored in `tool/meaning/meanings_a2.json` and the gate holds both levels at full coverage; (c) a first run on a phone, and the Python mirror that checks every model answer entails its statements once the model is in the repository; (d) **grammar**: a small grammar-correction model downloaded on request — the Settings row "Grammar check, offline" (coming soon) and the drain's `LocalJudgementService` seam are reserved for it; LanguageTool (ADR 0005) stays for the 59 marked writing tasks meanwhile; (e) then the embedding model and the model answers' signal role can be retired once A2 is covered.

## Open questions carried from PRODUCT.md

- Whether additional native languages are ever supported (non-content item 6) affects how hard-coded the Arabic/English assumption stays in the meantime; not a blocker for A1–C2 content phases, which are English-content-only regardless.
