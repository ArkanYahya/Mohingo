# Roadmap

## Vision

A self-directed adult Arabic-speaking learner can go from zero English (A1) to full proficiency (C2), through the same structured, level-by-level, four-skill progression established for the MVP — offline-first, bilingual, on one app.

## Content phases (A1 → C2)

Guided-learning-hour figures below are cumulative, standard CEFR/Cambridge estimates (from zero) — a guideline for scoping each phase's content volume, not a commitment to exact numbers.

| Phase | CEFR level(s) | Cumulative guided hours (reference) | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| MVP | A1 | ~70–100 | Feature-complete, Phase 10 (QA/performance/store-readiness/docs) in progress — see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md) and [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) |
| Phase 2 | A2 | ~160–200 | Content released, app work unfinished — steps 1, 2a and 3-6 of the level-design spec have landed (app foundations, current level + level lock, import, pilot unit, four authoring batches, release); **step 2b is all that's left**: Home, Tests, Review, Vocabulary, Grammar and the placement test still read A1 directly, so A2's capstones have no way in and twelve ARB strings still say "A1" to a learner studying A2 — see [ADR 0006](adr/0006-multiple-levels.md) and the [A2 level-design spec](superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-a2-level-design.md) |
| Phase 3 | B1 | ~350–400 | Not started |
| Phase 4 | B2 | ~500–600 | Not started |
| Phase 5 | C1 | ~700–800 | Not started |
| Phase 6 | C2 | ~1,000–1,200 | Not started |

Each content phase repeats the same shape as A1: break the level into thematic blocks derived from CEFR "can-do" statements for that level, author blocks/units/exercises across all 4 skills through the AI-generate-then-review pipeline, and ship the result as a new bundled asset (`assets/content/en/A2/...`, etc.) the same way A1 ships — no Firestore, no network sync, per the local-only v1 decision (see [ADR 0004](adr/0004-local-only-v1-no-firebase-backend.md)); the app-owned level catalogue (`kLevelCatalog` in `lib/content/catalog/level_catalog.dart`) is what makes adding a level purely additive there. But "just more content" understated what A2 actually needed first: a level building on an earlier one (so its lessons can reference A1 grammar/words/phrases), per-item level lookup (a screen finds an item's level from its id instead of assuming A1), and rendering for content fields the app had parsed but never shown — real app mechanics, landed as the A2 spec's step 1 ("app foundations") on `feature/a2-foundations`, followed by step 2a ("current level, level lock") on `feature/a2-current-level` — a learner-facing current level, a soft lock, and a browsable-but-closed preview, all for the Learn tab only.

A2's *content* phase understated its own work too. What the curriculum package produced fit A1's data contract but was not a course: one-line conversations, no present perfect anywhere in the level CEFR is named for, 80 listening activities against 232 reading, and 120 productive tasks whose `evaluation` was a retired rubric-name list the parser drops to `null` — so no speaking, writing or mediation answer could ever be graded. Steps 3-6 (`feature/a2-pilot-u1`) rewrote it unit by unit in the shipped asset, added the gates in `test/content/a2_*` that keep it rewritten, and flipped the catalogue to `available`. Budget a comparable authoring effort for B1, not a copy-and-level-up: what the generator produces is a starting point, not a shippable level.

What release did **not** cover, and what Phase 2 therefore still owes: step 2b, the rest of the multiple-levels UI. Home, Tests, Review, Vocabulary, Grammar and the placement test still call `courseContentProvider('a1')` directly, so a learner who has switched to A2 sees A1's continue card, A1's words and grammar, A1's tests, and copy that says "A1" in twelve places — including the "Finish A1" card at the foot of A2's own unit path. A2's Final Mission and Final Assessment exist in the bundle and are gated by `test/content/a2_capstones_test.dart`, but nothing opens them. See [ADR 0006](adr/0006-multiple-levels.md) for what's decided and landed, and the [A2 level-design spec](superpowers/specs/2026-09-19-a2-level-design.md) for the step-2b design. B1+ inherit the mechanism ADR 0006 establishes — each just declares the level it builds on — so later phases shouldn't need to repeat this app-mechanics step, only the content-authoring shape described above.

## Non-content roadmap (deferred from MVP)

These were explicitly scoped out of the MVP (see [PRODUCT.md](PRODUCT.md)) and are revisited here as the product matures, roughly in this order:

1. **Cloud-based AI grading for speaking/writing** — replaces/augments the on-device speech-to-text + rule-based writing checks with real AI scoring (e.g. via Firebase Cloud Functions calling an LLM), once the offline-only approach's limitations are felt. See the "Explicitly rejected" note in [TECH_STACK.md](TECH_STACK.md).
2. **CI/CD pipeline** — formalize build/release automation (Codemagic or GitHub Actions) once release cadence increases.
3. **Monetization** — no plan yet; revisit once there's a working, validated product across more than one level.
4. **Social/community features** — streaks-with-friends, leaderboards, etc. — only after the core solo learning experience is proven.
5. **Teacher/classroom tools** — a different user (teacher) and data model (groups/assignments); treat as a distinct sub-project with its own brainstorming pass, not a bolt-on.
6. **Additional native languages beyond Arabic** — would require generalizing the currently-fixed `nativeLanguage`/`targetLanguage` fields (already schema-flexible, see [DATA_MODEL.md](DATA_MODEL.md)) into a real localization system, plus re-authoring contrastive explanations per language pair.
7. **Analytics and crash reporting** — no telemetry SDK ships in v1, to keep the app free of any required network call or third-party data collection while it's fully local. Revisit once there's a concrete question telemetry would answer.
8. **Account-based sync and local backup export/import** — v1 stores progress locally only, with no way to move it between devices or recover it after an uninstall (see [ADR 0004](adr/0004-local-only-v1-no-firebase-backend.md)). A basic local export/import is the smaller near-term step; full account sync is the larger one, likely arriving together with item 1 above (both need the same account/backend groundwork).

## Open questions carried from PRODUCT.md

- Speaking/writing assessment quality is capped by the on-device/rule-based approach until non-content item 1 above is tackled — worth revisiting after A1 ships and real usage shows whether that's a real pain point.
- Whether additional native languages are ever supported (non-content item 6) affects how hard-coded the Arabic/English assumption stays in the meantime; not a blocker for A1–C2 content phases, which are English-content-only regardless.
